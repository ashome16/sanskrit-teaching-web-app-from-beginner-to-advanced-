# Lesson 4: Introduction to Vibhakti (The 8 Cases)

In English, word order is strict. If you swap "The boy saw the lion" to "The lion saw the boy", the meaning inverts completely. If you remove prepositions ("with", "from", "for"), the sentence collapses into nonsense.

Sanskrit works completely differently. Instead of using separate prepositional words or rigid word order, Sanskrit attaches miniature role-stickers directly to the end of nouns. These case endings are called **Vibhaktis (विभक्तयः)**.

---

## 1. Word Scrambling Freedom
Because case endings define the exact role of every word, word order in Sanskrit is flexible. Consider these three sentences:
1. **बालकः फलम् खादति** (*Bālakaḥ phalam khādati*)
2. **फलम् बालकः खादति** (*Phalam bālakaḥ khādati*)
3. **खादति बालकः फलम्** (*Khādati bālakaḥ phalam*)

All three sentences mean: **"The boy eats the fruit."**  
- **बालकः** (ending in **ः**) will always be the subject (doer).
- **फलम्** (ending in **म्**) will always be the object (eaten item).
- **खादति** will always be the verb (eats).

---

## 2. The 8 Cases and Kāraka Roles
Sanskrit recognizes **8 Vibhaktis**, mapped to fundamental semantic roles called **Kārakas**:

| Case (विभक्तिः) | Role (कारकम्) | English Question | Everyday Meaning | Example (Boy: बालक) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Prathamā (प्रथमा)** | Kartā (Subject) | *Who does it?* | The Doer | **बालकः** (The boy) |
| **2. Dvitīyā (द्वितीया)** | Karma (Object) | *Whom / What?* | Direct Object | **बालकम्** (To the boy) |
| **3. Tṛtīyā (तृतीया)** | Karaṇa (Means) | *By / With what?* | Instrument / Together with | **बालकेन** (By/with the boy) |
| **4. Caturthī (चतुर्थी)** | Sampradāna (Recipient) | *For / To whom?* | Purpose / Giving to | **बालकाय** (For the boy) |
| **5. Pañcamī (पञ्चमी)** | Apādāna (Source) | *From where?* | Separation / Origin | **बालकात्** (From the boy) |
| **6. Ṣaṣṭhī (षष्ठी)** | Sambandha (Relation) | *Whose?* | Possession ("of") | **बालकस्य** (Of the boy) |
| **7. Saptamī (सप्तमी)** | Adhikaraṇa (Location) | *Where / In what?* | Location ("in/on/at") | **बालके** (In the boy) |
| **8. Sambodhana (सम्बोधनम्)** | Āmantraṇa (Address) | *Hey!* | Calling / Addressing | **हे बालक!** (O boy!) |

---

## 3. The Three Grammatical Numbers (वचनानि)
[[figure:eight-by-three-vacanas]]
While modern European languages only have Singular (one) and Plural (more than one), Sanskrit has a precise intermediate form:
1. **Ekavacanam (एकवचनम्):** Exactly one (e.g. one boy)
2. **Dvivacanam (द्विवचनम्):** Exactly two (e.g. a pair of boys, two eyes, two hands)
3. **Bahuvacanam (बहुवचनम्):** Three or more (e.g. many boys)

This creates a complete grid of **8 Cases × 3 Numbers = 24 distinct forms** for every declined noun!

## 4. Practice Exercises
- Match each case to its meaning:
  - **बालकाय** → Case 4 (For the boy)
  - **बालकस्य** → Case 6 (Of the boy / The boy's)
  - **बालकात्** → Case 5 (From the boy)
  - **बालकेन** → Case 3 (By / with the boy)
