# सन्धि · Sandhi: How Sounds Join
## Svara Sandhi – The Architecture of Vowel Transitions

Imagine you have a tub of blue paint and a tub of yellow paint. If you place them right next to each other, they stay separate. But if they bump into each other and mix, they create a brand new color: green!

Sandhi is just your tongue mixing sound-paints to create a beautiful, smooth conversation.

## 1. How Sandhi Works (with English Examples)

Even though Sandhi is a major rule in languages like Sanskrit and Hindi, we actually do it in English all the time without realizing it! Here are a few ways sounds join together:

- The Blend (When two sounds mix into a new one): Try saying "Want" and "to" fast: "Wanna" (Want + to = Wanna). Or "Don't" and "you": "Don't-cha" (Don't + you = Don'tcha) — the 't' and 'y' joined hands to make a 'ch' sound!
- The Sound Switch (When one sound changes to match its neighbor): Say "Input". If you say it at normal speed, your lips prepare for the "p" sound early, making it sound like "Im-put"! The 'n' switched to an 'm' to make it easier for your mouth.

## 2. Sandhi in Sanskrit and Hindi

In Indian languages, Sandhi is so popular that people actually change the way they write the words, not just how they say them!

When two words are spoken in rapid succession in Sanskrit, their boundary sounds blend seamlessly. This phonetic blending is called Sandhi (literally, "joining" or "junction").

The first major branch of this science is Svara Sandhi (Vowel Transition). It dictates exactly what happens when a word ending in a vowel meets a word beginning with a vowel. Instead of a clunky pause, the two vowels mathematically fuse into a single, optimized sound.

## 3. The Core Mathematical Concept

Pāṇini’s system relies on the vowel weights we established in Article 1. When vowels combine, they generally upgrade into a Dīrgha (long vowel) or morph via structural vowel grading into a Guṇa or Vṛddhi vowel.

As a quick refresher from the Māheśvara Sūtras:

- Guṇa Vowels: ए (e), ओ (o), अर् (ar)
- Vṛddhi Vowels: ऐ (ai), औ (au), आर् (ār)

## 4. The 5 Essential Rules of Svara Sandhi

Here are the primary types of vowel transitions you will encounter, ordered by their mathematical logic:

## Rule 1: Dīrgha Sandhi (Homogeneous Vowel Prolongation)

When two of the same vowels (whether short or long) meet, they merge into their single, corresponding long vowel.

Formula: Similar Vowel + Similar Vowel → Long Vowel

- a / ā + a / ā → ā: देव + आलयः → देवालयः (deva + ālayas → devālayas / House of Gods)
- i / ī + i / ī → ī: कपी + ईशः → कपीशः (kapī + īśaḥ → kapīśaḥ / Lord of Monkeys)

## Rule 2: Guṇa Sandhi (The First-Degree Blend)

When an a or ā is followed by a different simple vowel (i, u, ṛ), they blend into a single Guṇa vowel.

Formula: a / ā + Different Vowel → Guṇa Vowel

- a / ā + i / ī → e: गण + ईशः → गणेशः (gaṇa + īśaḥ → gaṇeśaḥ / Lord of the Tribes)
- a / ā + u / ū → o: महा + उत्सवः → महोत्सवः (mahā + utsavaḥ → mahotsavaḥ / Great Festival)

## Rule 3: Vṛddhi Sandhi (The Second-Degree Growth)

When an a or ā meets an already complex diphthong (e, o, ai, au), the sound undergoes maximum expansion into a Vṛddhi vowel.

Formula: a / ā + Diphthong → Vṛddhi Vowel

- a / ā + e / ai → ai: तव + एव → तवैव (tava + eva → tavaiva / Yours alone)
- a / ā + o / au → au: महा + औषधिः → महौषधिः (mahā + auṣadhiḥ → mahauṣadhiḥ / Great Medicine)

## Rule 4: Yaṇ Sandhi (The Semi-Vowel Semi-Flip)

When any simple vowel other than a/ā (i, u, ṛ) is followed by any dissimilar vowel, the first vowel completely surrenders its vowel status and transforms into its corresponding semi-vowel consonant (y, v, r).

Formula: Simple Vowel (not a/ā) + Dissimilar Vowel → Semi-vowel + Dissimilar Vowel

- i / ī + dissimilar → y: इति + आदि → इत्यादि (iti + ādi → ityādi / Et cetera / And so on)
- u / ū + dissimilar → v: सु + आगतम् → स्वागतम् (su + āgatam → svāgatam / Welcome)

## Rule 5: Ayādi Sandhi (Diphthong Unrolling)

When compound vowels (e, o, ai, au) are followed by any vowel, they split apart into combinations of a semi-vowel and an open vowel:

- e → ay (e.g., ने + अनम् → नयनम् / ne + anam → nayanam / Eye)
- o → av (e.g., भो + अनम् → भवनम् / bho + anam → bhavanam / Building)

## 5. Quick Reference Matrix

| If Word 1 Ends In... | And Word 2 Starts With... | The Resulting Sound Is... | Sandhi Name |
| a / ā | a / ā | ā | Dīrgha |
| a / ā | i / ī | e | Guṇa |
| a / ā | u / ū | o | Guṇa |
| a / ā | e / ai | ai | Vṛddhi |
| i / ī | Any other vowel | y + [Word 2 Vowel] | Yaṇ |
| u / ū | Any other vowel | v + [Word 2 Vowel] | Yaṇ |
