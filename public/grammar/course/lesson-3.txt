# Lesson 3: The Concept of Roots (Dhātu) and Nouns (Prātipadika)

In Lessons 1 and 2, you mastered the phonetics of Sanskrit: 13 vowels and 33 consonants. Now, we step into the engineering room of Sanskrit grammar (Vyākaraṇa). 

Sanskrit is not a collection of arbitrary words memorized by rote. It is a generative linguistic system. Almost every word in the language is derived from a compact set of atomic seeds.

---

## 1. The Two Pillars of Sanskrit Words
[[figure:two-pada-pillars]]

In Sanskrit, every inflected word used in a sentence is called a **Pada (पदम्)**. Pāṇini’s golden rule states:
> **अपदं न प्रयुञ्जीत** (*Apadaṁ na prayuñjīta*)  
> *"Never use a raw, uninflected word in a sentence."*

All words fall into one of two fundamental categories:
1. **Nominal Words (Subanta - सुबन्त):** Nouns, pronouns, and adjectives that decline through case endings.
2. **Verbal Words (Tiṅanta - तिङन्त):** Verbs that conjugate through tense, person, and number endings.

---

## 2. Verbal Roots: Dhātu (धातु)
A **Dhātu** is the atomic root conveying an action or state of being. The Sanskrit lexicon contains approximately 2,000 primary roots. 

Common examples:
- **√पठ् (paṭh)**: To read, recite, study
- **√गम् (gam)**: To go, move, reach
- **√लिख् (likh)**: To write, record
- **√खाद् (khād)**: To eat
- **√पा (pā / pib)**: To drink
- **√भू (bhū)**: To become, exist

When a root receives conjugational endings (called **Tiṅ-pratyaya**), it becomes a live verb:
- **√पठ्** + **ति** → **पठति** (*paṭhati* = "He/She reads")
- **√गम्** + **ति** → **गच्छति** (*gacchati* = "He/She goes")

---

## 3. Nominal Bases: Prātipadika (प्रातिपदिक)
A **Prātipadika** is a raw nominal crude stem that carries a stable meaning, but has not yet received a case ending. 

Examples of nominal crude stems:
- **बालक** (*bālaka*) = boy (stem)
- **गज** (*gaja*) = elephant (stem)
- **लता** (*latā*) = creeper (stem)
- **फल** (*phala*) = fruit (stem)

You cannot drop the raw stem **बालक** directly into a Sanskrit sentence. It must receive a case ending (**Sup-pratyaya**) to clarify its role (e.g. who did the action, to whom, with what tool):
- **बालक** + **ः** → **बालकः** (*bālakaḥ* = "A boy", acting as subject)
- **बालक** + **म्** → **बालकम्** (*bālakam* = "To the boy", acting as object)

---

## 4. Word Modifiers: Upasargas (उपसर्गाः)
Sanskrit has **22 verbal prefixes** called **Upasargas**. When prefixed to a root, an Upasarga can dramatically transform or intensify its meaning:
- **√गम्** (*to go*)
  - **आ** + गम् → **आगच्छति** (*to come*)
  - **अनु** + गम् → **अनुगच्छति** (*to follow*)
  - **अधि** + गम् → **अधिगच्छति** (*to attain / comprehend*)

---

## 5. Practice Exercises
- **Root or Stem?** Identify whether each word is a verbal root (Dhātu) or a nominal stem (Prātipadika):
  1. **√खाद्** (Dhātu - to eat)
  2. **वृक्ष** (Prātipadika - tree)
  3. **√लिख्** (Dhātu - to write)
  4. **अश्व** (Prātipadika - horse)
- **Pada Transformation:** Notice the transformation:
  - Root **√पठ्** → Raw stem **पाठ** (lesson) → Finished word **पाठम्** (the lesson, as object).
