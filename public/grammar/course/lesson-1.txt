# Lesson 1: Introduction to Devanāgarī & Vowels (Svara)

Welcome to your first lesson in Sanskrit Grammar. Sanskrit is a highly structured, phonetic language. What you write is exactly what you speak. This lesson covers the script used for Sanskrit and its foundational sounds: the vowels.

---

## 1. The Script: Devanāgarī (देवनागरी)
Sanskrit is most commonly written in the **Devanāgarī** script. 
- **Deva** means "Divine/Gods".
- **Nāgarī** means "of the city".
- Together, it translates to the **"Script of the City of Gods"**.

Devanāgarī is written from **left to right**. A defining feature of this script is the horizontal top line (called **Shirorekha**) that binds the letters of a word together.

---

## 2. Introduction to Vowels: Svara (स्वर)
In Sanskrit grammar, vowels are called **Svara**. These are sounds that can be pronounced independently without the help of any other letter. 

Sanskrit traditionally recognizes **13 primary vowels**, which are categorized based on their duration of utterance (called **Mātrā**).

### A. Short Vowels (Hrasva Svara - ह्रस्व स्वर)
These take **one unit of time (1 Mātrā)** to pronounce. Think of them as short, crisp sounds.

- **अ** (a) - Sound like the 'a' in *about* or *bus*.
- **इ** (i) - Sound like the 'i' in *it* or *pin*.
- **उ** (u) - Sound like the 'u' in *put* or *full*.
- **ऋ** (ṛ) - A vocalic 'r' sound, similar to the 'ri' in *rhythm* or *rigid*.
- **ऌ** (ḷ) - A vocalic 'l' sound, rare in classical Sanskrit, similar to the 'le' in *table*.

### B. Long Vowels (Dīrgha Svara - दीर्घ स्वर)
These take **two units of time (2 Mātrās)** to pronounce. They are held twice as long as short vowels.

- **आ** (ā) - Sound like the 'a' in *father* or *spa*.
- **ई** (ī) - Sound like the 'ee' in *meet* or *see*.
- **ऊ** (ū) - Sound like the 'oo' in *boot* or *root*.
- **ॠ** (ṝ) - A prolonged vocalic 'r' sound (very rare).

### C. Diphthongs / Compound Vowels (Samyukta Svara - संयुक्त स्वर)
These are long vowels formed by combining two different vowel sounds together. They also take **two units of time (2 Mātrās)**.

- **ए** (e) - Sound like the 'ay' in *day* or 'e' in *cafe*. (Formed by अ + इ)
- **ऐ** (ai) - Sound like the 'ai' in *aisle* or 'y' in *my*. (Formed by अ + ए)
- **ओ** (o) - Sound like the 'o' in *go* or *boat*. (Formed by अ + उ)
- **औ** (au) - Sound like the 'ow' in *cow* or 'ou' in *house*. (Formed by अ + ओ)

---

## 3. The Modifiers (Ayogavāha - अयोगवाह)
There are two additional symbols that always follow a vowel and modify its sound:

- **Anusvāra (अं)**: Represented as a dot above the letter. It creates a nasal sound, like the 'm' or 'n' in *gong* or *hum*.
- **Visarga (अः)**: Represented as two vertical dots after the letter. It adds a soft, breathed echo of the preceding vowel (e.g., *ah*, *ih*, *uh*).

---

## 4. Practice Exercises
To master this lesson, practice the following:
- **Writing:** Copy each of the 13 vowels 5 times on a sheet of paper, ensuring you draw the top horizontal line last.
- **Pronunciation:** Practice alternating between short and long counterparts: **अ (a) vs आ (ā)**, **इ (i) vs ई (ī)**, **उ (u) vs ऊ (ū)**. Feel the length double for the long vowels.
